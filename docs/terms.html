2. What SafeDiscreet is — and is not

SafeDiscreet lets you capture video, photos and audio, mask the screen while you do it, add the time and place to what you captured, keep it in an encrypted vault on your device, and export it when you choose to.

Being explicit about the limits matters more here than in most apps:

  • It is not evidence-handling software. Whether a recording is admissible, relevant or persuasive anywhere is decided by the rules of that forum and by lawyers, not by this app.
  • The integrity check is not a certification. SafeDiscreet records a SHA-256 digest of each file at the moment it is saved. Re-checking it later tells you whether that file has changed since. It does not prove when, where or by whom the recording was made, and it is not issued or witnessed by any third party.
  • Location and time stamps are best-effort. They come from your device's own clock and location services, with the accuracy those provide at that moment. They can be wrong, coarse or absent.
  • Screen masking hides the screen, not the act. It covers the display while you capture. It does not silence the device, disable indicators iOS shows for camera or microphone use, or make a recording undetectable to anyone present.
  • It is not a personal-safety service. SafeDiscreet does not contact emergency services, does not alert anyone, and does not monitor you. In an emergency, call your local emergency number.

3. Your responsibilities when recording

You are solely responsible for ensuring that every recording you make is lawful where you make it. We cannot advise you on this, and nothing in the app should be read as saying a particular recording is permitted.

Recording law varies enormously between countries and, in some countries, between regions. Points that catch people out:

  • Consent. Some places allow a recording if one participant consents — often you. Others require every participant to consent. Recording a conversation you are part of may be lawful in one place and a criminal offence a border away.
  • Audio is usually treated more strictly than video, including in places where filming in public is unrestricted.
  • Place matters. Private homes, bathrooms, changing rooms, medical settings, schools, courts, police stations and secure facilities are commonly subject to specific prohibitions.
  • Workplaces are frequently governed by separate employment and data-protection rules, and by your own contract.
  • Publishing a lawful recording can still breach privacy, defamation or data-protection law.

If you are unsure, take legal advice before recording rather than after.
